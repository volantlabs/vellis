# Campaign-Plan Review

Review the plan independently of its author. Use fresh context when available and provide only the
current model, project bindings, and candidate campaign record—not an expected slice inventory or
prior diagnosis.

## Coverage and semantic closure

- Reconstruct the implementation-bearing authority universe from the complete model.
- Confirm every authority reference resolves in the active baseline and every planned contribution
  preserves its complete instance-level meaning.
- Confirm partial contributions identify all remaining slices and aggregate to full planned coverage.
- Find omitted negative commitments, failure non-effects, alternate behavior, and cross-cutting
  timing, concurrency, durability, recovery, safety, security, physical, or numerical semantics.
- Confirm jointly derived values and unified owners are not split into competing authorities.

## Dependency and execution adequacy

- Confirm dependency edges follow semantic prerequisites rather than declarations or presumed source
  layers.
- Confirm every slice is implementable and independently reviewable after its dependencies complete.
- Confirm each slice has discriminating evidence and an observable boundary.
- Confirm integration and closure slices exist only where assembled behavior has additional modeled
  obligations.
- Confirm the order retires consequential uncertainty before irreversible realization.

## Subtraction and authority boundary

- Remove slices that exist only for a conventional architecture, framework, future provider,
  speculative abstraction, documentation symmetry, or test inventory.
- Move necessary project setup into the semantic slice that first uses and proves it.
- Remove copied model prose, architecture prescriptions, stories, estimates, and source inventories
  from the campaign record.
- Keep implementation status, authority coverage, and campaign lifecycle distinct.

## Disposition

Classify findings as a language question, model gap, plan gap, realization decision, feasibility
consequence, implementation defect, stale baseline, or out-of-scope request. Repair plan defects and
repeat the entire review after material changes. Route consequential language questions to the
reference skill and genuine model gaps or stakeholder-visible feasibility consequences to model
work. A new or revised plan remains awaiting human approval even after reviewers find no material
issue.

A final cold reviewer must be able to identify the next slice, reread all authority it depends on,
explain why earlier slices are prerequisites, state what evidence will close it, and name the
whole-system completion conditions without prior conversation.
